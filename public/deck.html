01Video wall · Proof of conceptLive
The brief

Turn a spoken request into the right feed, and let the whole room trust what it sees

P1 · Pick the right feed

“Thermal feed from vehicle Alpha Two Zero on the big tile.” From what was said to the exact feed in seconds, out of 80 to 1,000 feeds.

P2 · Know what the wall shows

Operator and officers know what every tile is, without taking their eyes off what they are watching.

P3 · Reconfigure fast

Change tile sizes and positions, swap feeds, switch the OSD per tile, build, save and recall layouts.

Wall operator

Primary. Trained heavy user at the layout device, changes the wall on request, at shift change and when a feed fails.

HQ officers

Secondary. Read every tile from across the room on one 146″ screen, never touch the interface.

Out of scope in the brief: steering cameras, user accounts and permissions.

03Brief checklistLive
How I moved through the work

Research and framing first, then build and test in short rounds

1 · Understand

Read the brief. Desk research on operations rooms, CCTV attention and legibility at distance. Personas and workflows.

2 · Frame

Design brief: 7 problem statements, ideal states, 10 assumptions, 5 principles, 18 frictions, 8 test scenarios.

3 · Decode

Read the brief's OSD frame into a legend: 9 zones matched to the data table.

4 · Prototype

Built in rounds. I tested each round by hand and wrote notes; the next round answered them. Two desk layouts.

5 · Build

Moved to code. Wall on its own screen, OSD with mount context, a full keyboard model, automated test runs.

No field research was expected, so every bet is written down: as an assumption with its consequence if wrong, or as one of 19 open questions for Hevi.

07Desk and wallLive
Key decisions per problem
P1 · Pick the right feed
  • One search field for every identifier, including spoken call signs and partial grids
  • One row per system, DAY and THR side by side, each with its state
  • Grid asks ranked by distance
  • The target tile is shown before Enter; ⌥1–9 picks another
P2 · Know what the wall shows
  • The same label on the wall and the mirror: number, name and camera, state
  • State changes the whole band: LIVE, REC with time, FROZEN with the last frame
  • One focus tile; picture-in-picture both ways
  • OSD keeps all nine original zones and adds the mount: heading tape, pan and tilt dials, travel limits
P3 · Reconfigure fast
  • Five arrangements, saved layouts recalled in one action
  • Swap with S; OSD with O
  • Live or Staged, then Publish
  • Undo, redo, and restore any earlier state from Recent changes
  • A frozen or offline tile brings its replacements to the top of the list

10Demo run sheetLive
For the questions

What I would ask Hevi first

  • Which names do officers actually use for systems? (Q1)
  • How deep is the room? It sets the label size. (Q2)
  • Should a failed feed fall back on its own? (Q3)
  • Can the server tell a frozen feed from a live one? (Q4)
  • Is the layout device a desktop with a keyboard? (Q11)
  • Which mounts carry a rangefinder, and how far do they pan and tilt? (Q19)

How I would validate it

  • Run the eight scenarios with trained operators
  • Time to the correct feed against the scenario targets, and a wrong-feed rate of 0%
  • Officers name system, camera and state of a tile from the back of the room: at least 95% correct
  • Ease and workload per task and session: SEQ, SUS (target 80), NASA-TLX against today's tool
